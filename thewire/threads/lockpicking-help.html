BINARYFOX member 17:12
i ordered one of those cheap pick sets off amazon lol. it came with like a bunch of different shaped picks and two of those L shaped metal things. no idea what any of it is even for
CIPHER admin 17:19
The two L-shaped pieces are tension wrenches. Put one in the bottom of the keyway and apply light, steady rotational pressure — the same direction the key would turn. That's half the job. The picks go in above it to lift the pins one at a time. Start with a basic hook pick, not the rake. You want to learn the fundamentals first.
BINARYFOX member 17:24
ok wait how much pressure tho. i tried turning it and literally nothing happened
CIPHER admin 17:28
Less than you think. Too much tension and the pins bind up before you can seat them properly. Too little and they just fall back down the moment you lift them. You're feeling for the lightest pressure that still lets the cylinder want to rotate. It takes some trial and error before your hands understand what that feels like — that's normal.
BINARYFOX member 17:33
so like how do i even know when a pin is "set" vs not set
CIPHER admin 17:37
You'll feel a very slight give in the cylinder, sometimes a faint click, and the pin stays up on its own instead of springing back when you ease off it. Work them one at a time — usually whichever pin is binding hardest under your tension goes first. Raking scrubs across all of them at once and hoping something sets. It works occasionally on cheap hardware, but it won't teach you anything. Go slow.
