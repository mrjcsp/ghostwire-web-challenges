SNAKEBYTE member 14:02
ok i will say something embarrassing. i have a capture file open in wireshark and it is just. a wall of packets. thousands of lines. i write code all day, i do not do this. someone explain how anyone reads this without losing their mind
PACKETWOLF member 14:05
nobody reads all of it. that's mistake everyone makes the first time. you don't scroll, you filter. there's a bar at the top of the window, right under the toolbar. that's the display filter. type a protocol name in there and hit enter and it hides everything that isn't that protocol. the wall of packets you're looking at is probably TCP handshakes, ARP noise, DNS lookups, background traffic that has nothing to do with what you actually care about.
SNAKEBYTE member 14:07
ok so what do i actually type. give me the exact thing
PACKETWOLF member 14:09
type http, lowercase, nothing else, into the filter bar and hit enter. that shows you only HTTP traffic. if the capture has any plaintext web request in it at all, is where they show up. worth knowing why this even works: HTTP is unencrypted. every request, every response, every header, sitting right there in the packet in plain text. no decryption, no key, nothing. that's why people leave things in a capture without realizing it, they assume nobody's actually going to sit down and read through it. most people don't. we do.
SNAKEBYTE member 14:14
ok that actually cut it down to almost nothing, way better. now i have like 7 requests. i clicked one and it's showing me hex garbage on one side and something that looks like actual text on the other, is that the part i want
PACKETWOLF member 14:19
yeah, that's the packet bytes pane, you can read it there but is annoying. faster way: right click the packet, Follow, then HTTP Stream. that reconstructs the whole request and response as actual readable text in one window instead of you piecing it together packet by packet. once you're looking at the request, check the URL first, in first place. the request line at the top, right after GET or POST. people put things in query parameters they really shouldn't. that's usually the fastest place to look before you even bother with the headers or the response body.
